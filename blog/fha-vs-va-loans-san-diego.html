FHA vs VA Loans in San Diego:
Which Is Better for You?

A complete 2025 comparison — down payment, credit score, costs, and which loan wins for San Diego buyers

If you're buying a home in San Diego and trying to decide between an FHA loan and a VA loan, you're asking the right question. The answer depends on whether you're eligible for VA benefits — and if you are, the VA loan wins almost every time.

Here's a complete breakdown so you can make the right call for your situation.

VA Loans: The Clear Winner for Eligible Buyers

If you served in the military or are an active duty service member in San Diego — and San Diego has one of the largest military populations in the country — a VA loan is almost always the better choice.

Why VA beats FHA:

  • Zero down payment — In a market where the median home is $700,000+, saving 3.5% means $24,500+ out of pocket. VA saves you that entirely.
  • No mortgage insurance — FHA charges a monthly MIP that adds $200–$400/month to your payment. VA has none.
  • No loan limits — VA buyers with full entitlement can borrow as much as they qualify for, with no cap.
  • Competitive rates — VA loans typically have lower interest rates than FHA loans.
Example: On a $650,000 home in Chula Vista, a VA buyer saves roughly $22,750 in down payment and $350/month in mortgage insurance compared to FHA. Over 5 years that's over $43,000 in savings.

FHA Loans: The Best Option for Non-Veterans

FHA loans are the go-to for buyers who don't have VA eligibility. They're more flexible than conventional loans and allow buyers with lower credit scores or limited savings to get into a home.

FHA is a good fit if:

  • You're not a veteran or don't have VA eligibility
  • Your credit score is between 500–619
  • You have limited savings (3.5% down is the minimum)
  • You want a government-backed loan with predictable terms

The downside is mortgage insurance. With FHA you pay 1.75% upfront (can be rolled into the loan) plus a monthly premium for the life of the loan in most cases.

Can You Use Both in San Diego?

You can only use one loan at a time to purchase a home. However, veterans who have used their VA benefit before may be able to restore entitlement after selling their prior home, or use remaining entitlement alongside a new VA loan in some cases.

Bottom Line

  • Veteran or active military? → Use your VA loan. Full stop.
  • Not military-eligible? → FHA is likely your best path if your score is under 680 or you have limited savings.
  • Strong credit and 10–20% down? → Consider conventional — you can drop mortgage insurance at 20% equity.
